# Course Knowledge Card Review

## Scope

The 3000-question student practice bank has been retired. This release contains 453 concise recall cards across S1-S10, split between core concepts and detail knowledge. Each card tests one point, gives a short explanatory answer, and carries a course-module or source-page label. S2 retains 172 cards checked against the supplied V04 Study Pack pages 31-52.

This is a topic-and-answer review of the 453-card release against the supplied course materials, not a claim that every card is a verbatim standard clause or that every installation rule has been independently certified. The legacy module lessons/video scripts remain supplementary; where they contain unsupported fixed values (notably some S8/S9 testing or installation figures), do not treat those figures as general requirements. The card set intentionally omits them unless tied to a clearly identified course page and caveat.

## Review approach

- Reorganized the course around definitions, mechanisms, selection criteria, calculations, common confusions, verification evidence and safe-work boundaries.
- Split multi-part answers into smaller recall prompts where practical; retained grouped lists only when the list itself is the knowledge target.
- Removed unsupported universal anchor loads, safety factors, conduit support distances, cable fill assumptions and fixed pass/fail thresholds from the new card set.
- Reframed values that depend on standards, equipment, substrate or installation conditions as items to verify in the current standard, product data or site procedure.
- Kept course-stated values on S2 cards with explicit source-page labels; use them as textbook recall, not as a substitute for current workshop/tool instructions.
- Avoided instructions that could be mistaken for permission to perform live electrical work. Isolation and testing cards emphasize authorization and verification.

## Module map

- S1: risk assessment, hazard/risk distinction, electrical and non-electrical hazards, isolation/LOTO, proving test instruments, CAT/IP concepts, arc flash, hazardous materials and construction-site controls.
- S2: measurement/marking, saws/chisels/snips/files, sheet metal, drill selection and speeds, hole saws, metric threads, tap-drill calculations, taps/dies/extractors, power tools, dismantling, soldering/brazing and MIG concepts.
- S3: fastener/fixing choice, substrate and load direction, expansion/chemical/hollow-wall anchors, supports, adhesives, environmental/heritage considerations and installation inspection.
- S4: drawing types and scales, symbols/legends, schedules, layout versus circuit logic, one-/two-way/intermediate lighting, polarity, neutral and protective earth concepts, construction coordination.
- S5: control versus power circuits, NO/NC contacts, relay coils, contactors, latching, electrical/mechanical interlocks and evidence-based control troubleshooting.
- S6: rotating magnetic field, synchronous speed/slip, nameplate interpretation, DOL and reversing starters, overload versus short-circuit protection, motor symptoms and post-repair verification.
- S7: conductor material/stranding/area, insulation and cable layers, terminal compatibility, stripping/crimping/torque, enclosure edges, mechanical support and identification.
- S8: verification sequence, visual checks, protective continuity, insulation resistance, polarity/phase sequence, RCD checks, measurement limitations, records and half-split fault finding.
- S9: enclosure/environment matching, conduit cutting and joints, bending defects, support criteria, route coordination, pull points, moisture/fire barriers and cable protection.
- S10: load current, single/three-phase relationships, demand factors, cable derating, voltage drop, protective coordination, interrupting capacity and design records.

## Important limits

This is a learning aid, not an installation design or compliance certificate. Standard numbers and requirements can change and depend on jurisdiction. For real work, confirm the current adopted standards, approved drawings, manufacturer instructions, site procedures and licensed supervision. Calculations involving demand, cable capacity, fault protection or RCD performance must be independently checked against the applicable source tables and installation conditions.
